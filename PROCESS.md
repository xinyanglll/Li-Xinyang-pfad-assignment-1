# Process

## How I Used AI

I used ChatGPT as an AI assistant while developing this essay.

I mainly used it for brainstorming, organising my ideas, drafting English paragraphs, and editing the structure of my writing. I first provided my own experiences and thoughts, including my previous experience using AI-generated code for a pet health recognition feature and my reaction to the Schotter exercise. I then used ChatGPT to help organise these ideas into a clearer argument in English.

I did not ask AI to generate the entire essay without reviewing it. I read the suggestions, changed parts that did not accurately represent my experience, and decided which ideas and wording to keep.

## One AI Output I Kept

One idea from ChatGPT that I kept was the statement:

> "Programming gives me another material to think and design with."

I kept this idea because it describes the change in my understanding after doing the Schotter exercise. Before the course, I mainly thought of code as something that makes a system function. The Schotter exercise showed me that code can also generate visual forms and that changing a simple parameter can change the final result. This idea helped me connect programming with my existing understanding of design materials.

## One AI Output I Rejected

One AI-generated version described my previous project with more specific details than I wanted to include. Although the paragraph sounded plausible and well written, I rejected it because it introduced details that did not accurately represent the experience I wanted to discuss.

I changed the description to focus on what I actually experienced: working on a pet health AI recognition feature and using AI to generate the code based on my design requirements. This was important because a paragraph can sound convincing without necessarily being my argument or accurately representing my experience.

This also became part of the reason I think learning programming matters. If I cannot understand or question what an AI produces, it is much harder to notice when an output sounds correct but is actually wrong for my purpose.
